## Ask 2: Scarcity / solitude line per open-access site

Applied to **Ballochroy, Drombeg, Clava Cairns** only — the genuinely quiet, open, unmanaged sites. NOT Stonehenge/Newgrange/Maeshowe. Each line says what visiting is *actually like* given that site's documented access.

**Ballochroy** (grounded: working farm, unsignposted track, 10–20 min uphill, stones not visible till you're near, gates closed, dogs on leads):
> "No visitor centre, no signs on the road, no one else required to be here. Gates closed, farm track, ten minutes on your own. The stones are behind you until you're actually near them."

**Drombeg** (grounded: small car park ~10 cars, 2–5 min maintained path, often wet, exposed):
> "A small car park at the end of a lane and a short path. On most visits you're the only one on it. Wind, wet underfoot, and the stones to yourself."

**Clava Cairns** (grounded: open access, free, no booking; cemetery-style site among the cairns):
> "Free, open, no booking — it reads quiet from the lane, and it's quieter once you're among the cairns. Nothing here asks you to hurry."

Each reads as **invitation, not warning** — no invented numbers, no comparisons to other sites, no "best time/quietest day" invention. Stays true to the honesty rule: the register's "saw nothing" stays a valid entry, these lines just make the getting-there solitude legible.
